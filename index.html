02 · Flagship Project

PolarNOx — NASA Sounding Rocket Mission

A NASA-funded Virginia Tech mission measuring nitric oxide buildup in the polar night upper atmosphere, using a UV spectrograph to track starlight dimming as the rocket's line of sight passed through the NO layer.

ROLE  Instrumentation, Calibration & Data Analysis
SITE  Poker Flat Research Range, AK
LAUNCH  Jan 30, 2026 · 04:20 AKST
Problem

The mission's spectrograph relied on an automated star tracker to hold a reference star in view. A tracker failure mid-flight would end the science — the payload needed a manual backup, and the team needed to know exactly when to launch.

Approach

Calibrated the spectrograph and telescope pre-flight; built a manual backup aiming system using directional (up/down/left/right) commands to re-center the star; wrote a Python model of moon-star-rocket geometry to find the best launch window.

Tools

Python, Astropy, optical instrumentation & calibration procedures, manual telescope pointing/control, celestial geometry modeling.

Result

Successful launch on Jan 30, 2026. The spectrograph tracked the target star cleanly throughout flight and returned a full NO altitude density profile now under analysis.

LAUNCH WINDOW OPTIMIZATION Python / Astropy geometry model

Two candidate windows (Jan 17–21 and Jan 26–Feb 1) were identified where the moon–star–rocket angle stayed above 90°, minimizing stray-light contamination during peak nitric oxide abundance. Jan 30 was selected as the launch date; the mission flew that morning.
